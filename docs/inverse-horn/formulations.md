# Mathematical formulations and their unresolved choices

These are additive problems. A connection to the multiplicative unitary
problem in `can_sandwich` still requires a separate construction theorem.

## Endpoint problem and exact computability

For decreasing traceless real four-tuples alpha,beta,gamma, seek

    A=D_alpha, B=Q D_beta Q^T, Q in SO(4), spec(A+B)=gamma.

Real symmetric feasibility suffices for the endpoint existence question.
The determinant of Q can be changed by changing one eigenvector's sign,
without changing B. This does not parameterize all complex Hermitian pairs.

With M=A+B, trace is automatic. Newton identities show that

    tr(M²)=sum gamma_i², tr(M³)=sum gamma_i³, det(M)=prod gamma_i

determine the remaining characteristic coefficients. Along with Q^TQ=I
and det Q=1, these are a real polynomial system. For algebraic input data,
general real-algebraic sample-point algorithms such as CAD terminate and
return an algebraic witness when feasible. A rational-univariate method
needs an appropriate sample-point reduction for this positive-dimensional
set; a zero-dimensional solver alone is not that reduction.

This establishes exact computability in principle, not an efficient new
Horn construction, a radicals-only formula, or a practical implementation.
Approximate construction via Franks is a separate existing result.

## Hive dimensions and rank-three spectral chains

Fixing a generic sum spectrum imposes n-1 conditions on SO(n), leaving

    n(n-1)/2-(n-1)=(n-1)(n-2)/2

dimensions. This equals the generic hive dimension for every n. A dimension
count alone gives neither an inverse nor a useful correspondence.

Shift beta by its smallest value:

    delta_i=beta_i-beta4, Gamma=gamma-beta4*(1,1,1,1).

Then delta4=0, and the reconstruction is equivalent to finding orthonormal
v,w,u for

    D_alpha+delta1 vv^T+delta2 ww^T+delta3 uu^T

with spectrum Gamma. Write the two intermediate spectra as rho,tau.

In the Pak–Vallejo LR convention for type (Gamma,alpha,delta),

    a_0j=alpha_j,
    Gamma_j=alpha_j+sum_{i=1}^j a_ij,
    delta_i=sum_{j=i}^4 a_ij.

Adding letters successively defines

    rho_j=alpha_j+a_1j,
    tau_j=alpha_j+a_1j+a_2j, with a_2j=0 for j<2.

These spectra have the correct traces and positive rank-one interlacings.
They also satisfy rho1=tau1=Gamma1 and tau2=Gamma2. Writing
x=rho2, y=rho3, z=tau3, the chain is

    rho=(Gamma1,x,y,sum(alpha)+delta1-Gamma1-x-y),
    tau=(Gamma1,Gamma2,z,sum(alpha)+delta1+delta2-Gamma1-Gamma2-z).

The hive slopes give x=h12-h11, y=h13-h12, z=h23-h22. LR lattice-word
inequalities remain additional constraints; an arbitrary interlacing chain
is not automatically a hive. These chain coordinates do not automatically
make the rank-one directions orthogonal.

### Explicit orthogonality coupling

Let U1 diagonalize the first update, and U2 diagonalize the second update
in the U1 basis. Set V=U1^T v, z=U1^T w, W=U2^T z, y=U2^T U1^T u.
Here z,y are vector coordinates, separate from the hive scalars above.
Define a_j=V_j z_j and b_k=W_k y_k, also separate from LR entries a_ij.
For simple disjoint intermediate spectra,

    E12=sum_j a_j,
    E23=sum_k b_k,
    E13=delta2 sum_jk a_j b_k/(tau_k-rho_j).

Residues determine their squared magnitudes:

    a_j²=-p_alpha(rho_j)p_tau(rho_j)
          /(delta1 delta2 p_rho'(rho_j)²),
    b_k²=-p_rho(tau_k)p_Gamma(tau_k)
          /(delta2 delta3 p_tau'(tau_k)²).

The natural LR chain has common roots, so the unrestricted formula cannot
be evaluated by assigning values to 0/0. Its properly restricted form and
the necessary-and-sufficient strict-chain test are proved in
[rank3-coupling.md](archive/scouting/2026-09-15-additive-inverse-horn/rank3-coupling.md).
That test includes an actual backward/forward reconstruction, but supplies
no endpoint-to-chain selection or coverage of all degeneracies.

## Signed-singular-value formulation

For a traceless four-vector lambda, define

    L(lambda) = (lambda1-lambda2+lambda3-lambda4,
                 -lambda1+lambda2+lambda3-lambda4,
                 lambda1+lambda2-lambda3-lambda4)/4.

Write a=L(alpha), b=L(beta), c=L(gamma). In a compatible magic/Bell basis,
the diagonal matrix with eigenvalues alpha corresponds to

    H(a)=a1 X⊗X+a2 Y⊗Y+a3 Z⊗Z,

whose four Bell eigenvalues are

    (a1-a2+a3, -a1+a2+a3, a1+a2-a3, -a1-a2-a3).

The SO(4) action lifts to SU(2)×SU(2). The corresponding Bloch rotations
R,S lie in SO(3), and the sum corresponds to the correlation tensor

    T=D_a+R D_b S^T.

A proper SVD has T=R_c D_d S_c^T with both rotations proper. The absolute
values |d_i| are singular values and prod d_i=det T. The target condition
is d in the signed-permutation orbit of c generated by permutations and
even sign changes. This preserves the sign of the determinant.

Equivalently, the required invariants are

    tr(T^T T)=sum c_i²,
    ((tr(T^T T))²-tr((T^T T)²))/2=sum_{i<j} c_i² c_j²,
    det T=prod c_i.

Lifting R,S back through SU(2)×SU(2) reconstructs Q. Thus this is an exact
inverse-problem equivalence, not a selection procedure. The attempted
conversion to ordinary 3x3 Hermitian Horn by signs is false:
a=b=(3,2,1), c=(3sqrt(2),2sqrt(2),2) give a feasible tensor sum but no
possible additive signed trace. See the
[exact counterexample](archive/attempts/2026-09-15-R389-signed-hermitian/attempt.md).

## Quartic completion

Set P=(A+B)/2 and R=(A-B)/2 and consider p(x,y)=det(xI-P-yR).
The three spectral slices are alpha at y=1, beta at y=-1, and gamma/2 at
y=0. Since traces vanish,

    p=x^4+a(y)x²+b(y)x+c(y),
    deg a<=2, deg b<=3, deg c<=4.

For chi_lambda=x^4+a_lambda x²+b_lambda x+c_lambda, define

    I[r-,r0,r+](y)=r0+(r+-r-)*y/2+((r++r-)/2-r0)*y².

Then, with phi=y(y²-1), every permitted completion has

    a(y)=I[a_beta,a_gamma/4,a_alpha](y),
    b(y)=b0(y)+s phi(y), b0=I[b_beta,b_gamma/8,b_alpha],
    c(y)=c0(y)+phi(y)(t+uy), c0=I[c_beta,c_gamma/16,c_alpha].

Thus p=q+phi(sx+t+uy), where q=x^4+a x²+b0 x+c0 is fixed. Exactly three
coefficients remain. Feasibility requires four real x roots at every real y.

For a depressed quartic, the Hermite matrix is

    H = [[4, 0, -2a, -3b],
         [0, -2a, -3b, 2a²-4c],
         [-2a, -3b, 2a²-4c, 5ab],
         [-3b, 2a²-4c, 5ab, -2a³+6ac+3b²]].

All roots are real iff H is positive semidefinite. For simple roots,
positive definiteness is equivalent to

    a<0, 2a³-8ac+9b²<0, Delta>0,
    Delta=256c³-128a²c²+144ab²c+16a^4 c-27b^4-4a³b².

For repeated roots one must use PSD or an equivalent non-strict criterion;
replacing strict leading-minor inequalities by weak ones is not sufficient
in general. The critical-value interval in R390 handles multiplicities.

### Candidate singular boundary

For a finite collision at y outside {-1,0,1}, global real-rootedness requires
p=p_x=p_y=0. Solving these equations gives the supplied rational surface

    s=-q_x/phi,
    u=(phi' q-phi q_y)/phi²,
    t=(x q_x-q)/phi-yu.

This parameterizes candidates, not a declaration that every point on the
surface is a feasible completion. It also does not treat interpolation-line
degeneracies or classify the projective boundary. No map from hive facets
to the feasible part of this surface was constructed.

### What was proved about the feasible side

For fixed s, solve the derivative cubic 4x³+2a(y)x+b_s(y)=0. Its three
ordered real critical points r1<=r2<=r3 exist exactly when

    -8a(y)³-27b_s(y)²>=0.

Let K(r)=3r^4+a(y)r², L_s(y)=K(r2), U_s(y)=min(K(r1),K(r3)). Then

    L_s(y)-c0(y)<=phi(y)(t+uy)<=U_s(y)-c0(y)

is necessary and sufficient for the quartic at that y to be real-rooted,
including repeated roots. For each fixed s, these are halfplanes in (t,u).
Their intersection K_s is compact and convex. The constraints at y=2,3
bound independent linear combinations. Compactness plus planar Helly
gives an empty-slice certificate with at most three scalar inequalities.
The locations of those constraints must still be found; no universal grid
or three-test rule follows. The derivative-admissible s set is a compact
interval, but the successful-s projection is not proved to be an interval.

See the [full proof and limits](archive/attempts/2026-09-15-R390-quartic-slices/attempt.md).
Cao–Woerdeman's displayed factorization route constructs Hermitian
representations. Real symmetric existence and a directly executable real
construction must not be conflated.

## Proposed tridiagonal normal form

Simultaneous unitary tridiagonalization of two Hermitian 4x4 matrices is
established. The stronger proposal T4 fixes C=D_(pi gamma) diagonal and
requires A real tridiagonal. It is false even after all permutations.

Take A=J-I, C=diag(3,1,-1,-3), B=C-A. Every candidate with spectrum
(3,-1,-1,-1) is 4vv^T-I. Tridiagonality forces v to have support on at
most two neighboring coordinates. B must then retain at least two of
{4,2,0,-2}, while its prescribed polynomial x^4-16x²+8x+16 vanishes at
none of them. Closedness also gives nearby feasible all-simple exclusions.
The [proof and exact checks](archive/attempts/2026-09-15-R391-tridiagonal-sum/attempt.md)
cover every candidate and every permutation.
